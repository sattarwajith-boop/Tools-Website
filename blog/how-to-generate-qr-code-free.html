How to Generate a QR Code for Free in Your Browser

Published by ToolNest Studio · Updated May 19, 2026 · 6 minute read

QR codes are an easy way to connect printed material with digital information. A QR code can open a website, share contact details, show a menu, link to classroom notes, or send readers to a portfolio page. You can create one directly in your browser without installing software.

Students can use QR codes on project posters, revision sheets, presentations, and event notices. Small businesses can use them on flyers, product packaging, invoices, and shop counters. Bloggers and creators can use them for portfolios, link pages, newsletters, or downloadable resources.

What can a QR code contain?

The most common QR code content is a website link. Paste the URL, generate the code, download it, and test it. QR codes can also store plain text, phone numbers, email addresses, Wi-Fi details, short instructions, or contact information. The exact options depend on the generator.

Step-by-step method

  1. Decide what the QR code should do.
  2. Paste the link or text into a QR code generator.
  3. Choose the size and design if options are available.
  4. Download the QR code as an image.
  5. Test it with more than one phone before publishing.
  6. Place it where people can scan it easily.

Real-world example

A teacher preparing a printed revision sheet can add a QR code at the bottom of the page. Students scan it and open the extra resource instantly. A cafe can use a QR code for a menu. A student can add one to an exhibition board so visitors can read the full report online.

Design tips

Keep white space around the QR code. Do not place it too close to other text or images. Use strong contrast, usually dark code on a light background. Avoid making it too small. A good-looking QR code is not useful if it cannot be scanned quickly.

Important: Test your QR code after downloading and again after printing. Some QR codes look fine on screen but fail when printed too small.

Common mistakes

One common mistake is using a very long link without checking it. Another is changing or deleting the destination page after printing the code. Static QR codes cannot update themselves. If the printed QR code points to a page that later disappears, the printed material becomes less useful.

FAQ

Do QR codes expire? A normal static QR code does not expire, but the page it opens can stop working if removed.

Can I use a QR code for business? Yes. Many businesses use QR codes for menus, contact details, offers, and product information.

Should I add a label? Yes. Add text like “Scan for menu” or “Scan to download notes” so users know what to expect.
